People Judge Your Face in a Tenth of a Second

People decide whether you look trustworthy in about a tenth of a second, and more time barely changes their mind.

Transcript

People decide if you look trustworthy faster than you can blink.

In two thousand six, Janine Willis and Alexander Todorov at Princeton flashed photos of faces for just a tenth of a second.

People judged how trustworthy, likable and competent each face looked.

Then others judged the same faces with no time limit.

The quick judgments matched the slow ones closely. Trustworthiness was the fastest.

More time mostly just made people more confident in their first impression.

Important: these snap judgments are often wrong. They tell you about perception, not about who someone really is.

You cannot stop people from judging fast. But you can stop yourself from trusting your own snap judgment so much.

The science

Based on first impressions from faces (Willis and Todorov, 2006).

Willis, J., & Todorov, A. (2006). First impressions: Making up your mind after a 100-ms exposure to a face. Psychological Science, 17(7), 592-598. Accuracy of such judgments is limited (Todorov et al., 2015).

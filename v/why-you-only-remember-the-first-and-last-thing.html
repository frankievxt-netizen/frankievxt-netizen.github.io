Why You Only Remember the First and Last Thing

In any list, meeting or date, your brain keeps the beginning and the end and drops the middle.

Transcript

Your brain keeps the beginning and the end, and quietly throws away the middle.

In nineteen sixty two, Bennet Murdock read lists of words to people and asked them to recall as many as they could.

Words at the start and at the end were remembered best. The middle sank.

This is the serial position effect. Early items get rehearsed more, and the last items are still fresh.

It is why you remember the first and last song at a concert.

It is why in a job interview, going first or last can stand out more than going in the middle.

And why the end of a date can matter as much as the first five minutes.

So if something matters, do not bury it in the middle. Say it first, or say it last.

The science

Based on the serial position effect (Murdock, 1962).

Murdock, B. B. (1962). The serial position effect of free recall. Journal of Experimental Psychology, 64(5), 482-488. Primacy also linked to rehearsal (Rundus, 1971).

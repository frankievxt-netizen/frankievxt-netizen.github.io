The Dunning-Kruger Effect Isn't What You Think

The famous chart about stupid people being overconfident is not quite what the original study found.

Transcript

You have seen the meme. Stupid people think they are geniuses. That is not quite what the study found.

In nineteen ninety nine, David Dunning and Justin Kruger tested students on logic, grammar and humor.

Those who scored lowest overestimated their results the most. They thought they were above average.

But top scorers slightly underestimated themselves. And almost everyone thought they were above average.

Later, statisticians showed that part of this pattern appears even with random data, because of how it was measured.

Newer studies suggest a weaker version is real. People with less skill do tend to misjudge themselves.

But it does not mean confident people are dumb. It means everyone is bad at grading themselves.

If the Dunning Kruger effect made you think of someone else, you might be the example.

The science

Based on the Dunning Kruger effect (Kruger and Dunning, 1999) and its statistical critique.

Kruger, J., & Dunning, D. (1999). Unskilled and unaware of it. JPSP, 77(6), 1121-1134. Critiques: Nuhfer et al. (2016, 2017), Numeracy; Gignac & Zajenkowski (2020), Intelligence, 80, 101449. Jansen, Rafferty & Griffiths (2021) argue a smaller real effect remains.

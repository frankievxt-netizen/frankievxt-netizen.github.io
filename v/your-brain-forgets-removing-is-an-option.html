Your Brain Forgets Removing Is an Option

When people try to improve something, they almost always add to it, even when taking away would work better.

Transcript

When you want to fix something, your brain almost never thinks of removing.

In twenty twenty one, Leidy Klotz and his colleagues gave people a Lego structure with a wobbly roof.

Adding a brick cost money. Removing one brick fixed it for free.

Most people added bricks anyway. The simplest fix never even crossed their minds.

Across many tasks, from editing essays to improving a university, people kept choosing to add.

Subtracting only came to mind when people were reminded it was an option, or had time to think it through.

It is why your calendar fills up, your apps multiply, and your to do list never shrinks.

Before you add one more thing, ask: what could I remove instead?

The science

Based on overlooking subtractive changes (Adams et al., 2021).

Adams, G. S., Converse, B. A., Hales, A. H., & Klotz, L. E. (2021). People systematically overlook subtractive changes. Nature, 592, 258-261.

Do Lottery Winners Really End Up Unhappy?

You have heard lottery winners are no happier, but bigger and newer studies tell a different story.

Transcript

You have heard that lottery winners end up no happier. That is only half true.

In nineteen seventy eight, Philip Brickman studied twenty two lottery winners in Illinois.

They were not much happier than people who had not won. The study became famous as proof that money cannot buy happiness.

The idea behind it is hedonic adaptation. You get used to good things, and your happiness drifts back.

But in twenty twenty, Erik Lindqvist and his team studied thousands of Swedish lottery players over many years.

Big winners reported clearly higher life satisfaction, even more than a decade later.

Their day to day mood did not change much. But their sense that life was going well did.

Money does not make every day brighter. It may make the whole picture feel safer.

The science

Based on hedonic adaptation (Brickman et al., 1978; Lindqvist, Östling and Cesarini, 2020).

Brickman, P., Coates, D., & Janoff-Bulman, R. (1978). Lottery winners and accident victims: Is happiness relative? JPSP, 36(8), 917-927. Lindqvist, E., Östling, R., & Cesarini, D. (2020). Long-run effects of lottery wealth on psychological well-being. Review of Economic Studies, 87(6), 2703-2726.

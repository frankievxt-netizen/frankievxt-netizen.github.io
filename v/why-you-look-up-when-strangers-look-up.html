Why You Look Up When Strangers Look Up

One person staring at the sky gets ignored, but fifteen people staring stops the whole street.

Transcript

One person staring at the sky gets ignored. A crowd staring stops the street.

In nineteen sixty nine, Stanley Milgram and his colleagues placed people on a busy New York sidewalk, staring up at a window.

When one person stared, a few passersby stopped, though many glanced up.

When fifteen people stared, about forty percent of passersby stopped, and most looked up.

This is social proof. When you are not sure what is happening, you copy what others are doing.

It is why a restaurant with a line feels better than an empty one next door.

It is why reviews, likes and laugh tracks work so well.

Crowds are often right. But sometimes everyone is looking up at nothing.

The science

Based on social proof (Milgram, Bickman and Berkowitz, 1969).

Milgram, S., Bickman, L., & Berkowitz, L. (1969). Note on the drawing power of crowds of different size. Journal of Personality and Social Psychology, 13(2), 79-82. Social proof as an influence principle: Cialdini (1984).

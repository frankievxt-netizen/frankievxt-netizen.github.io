Why Copying Someone's Words Makes Them Like You

Waitresses who repeated customers' orders word for word earned noticeably bigger tips.

Transcript

Repeat someone's words back to them, and they will like you more.

In two thousand three, Rick van Baaren and his colleagues studied a waitress in a Dutch restaurant.

Sometimes she repeated the customer's order exactly as they said it. Other times she just said okay or coming right up.

When she repeated the order word for word, she got noticeably bigger tips, and more people tipped.

This is behavioral mimicry. When someone mirrors you, your brain reads it as a sign of similarity and connection.

Related studies found mimicry also makes people more helpful afterward.

You do it naturally with people you like, copying their posture, words and laugh.

Next time, try using someone's exact words. Just do it naturally, not like a robot.

The science

Based on verbal mimicry (van Baaren et al., 2003).

van Baaren, R. B., Holland, R. W., Steenaert, B., & van Knippenberg, A. (2003). Mimicry for money: Behavioral consequences of imitation. Journal of Experimental Social Psychology, 39(4), 393-398. Helpfulness: van Baaren et al. (2004), Psychological Science, 15(1), 71-74.

Learning Styles Are a Myth

Being a visual or auditory learner sounds true, but teaching to your learning style does not help you learn.

Transcript

Are you a visual learner? Science says that label might not matter.

Most teachers believe students learn better when lessons match their style: visual, auditory or hands on.

In two thousand eight, Harold Pashler and his colleagues reviewed the evidence.

They found almost no well designed studies showing that matching your style improves learning.

People do have preferences. But preferring pictures does not mean you learn more from pictures.

What really works is matching the method to the content: maps for geography, sound for languages.

And techniques that work for almost everyone, like testing yourself and spacing out your study.

You are not stuck with one way of learning. You can learn almost anything in many ways.

The science

Based on the learning styles myth (Pashler et al., 2008).

Pashler, H., McDaniel, M., Rohrer, D., & Bjork, R. (2008). Learning styles: Concepts and evidence. Psychological Science in the Public Interest, 9(3), 105-119. Belief prevalence: Dekker et al. (2012), Frontiers in Psychology.

A Notification Breaks Focus Even If You Ignore It

Just hearing your phone buzz can disrupt your focus about as much as picking it up.

Transcript

Ignoring a notification does not save your focus. The damage is already done.

In twenty fifteen, Cary Stothart and his team at Florida State gave people a task that needed steady attention.

During the task, some received calls or texts on their own phones. They did not answer them.

Their mistakes still went up clearly.

The researchers compared the effect to what happens when people actually use their phone.

A buzz starts a chain of thoughts. Who is it, is it important, should I check? Your mind leaves the task, even if your hands stay.

It is why you reread the same paragraph three times when your phone keeps lighting up.

Real focus starts with silence. Turn notifications off, not just down.

The science

Based on notification interruption (Stothart, Mitchum and Yehnert, 2015).

Stothart, C., Mitchum, A., & Yehnert, C. (2015). The attentional cost of receiving a cell phone notification. Journal of Experimental Psychology: Human Perception and Performance, 41(4), 893-897.

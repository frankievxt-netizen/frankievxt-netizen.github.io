Handwritten Notes Aren't the Superpower You Heard

The famous study saying handwritten notes beat laptops did not hold up as well when others tried it.

Transcript

Writing notes by hand makes you smarter than typing. You have heard it. It is not that clear.

In twenty fourteen, Pam Mueller and Daniel Oppenheimer found students who wrote by hand understood lectures better.

Their idea: typing lets you copy words without thinking, while writing forces you to summarize.

The study went viral. Schools even banned laptops.

But in twenty nineteen, Kayla Morehead and her colleagues repeated it closely. The difference mostly disappeared.

The real lesson is about how you take notes, not what you take them with.

If you rewrite ideas in your own words, you remember more on any device.

So keep your laptop. Just stop typing like a court reporter.

The science

Based on handwriting versus laptop notes (Mueller and Oppenheimer, 2014; Morehead, Dunlosky and Rawson, 2019).

Mueller, P. A., & Oppenheimer, D. M. (2014). The pen is mightier than the keyboard. Psychological Science, 25(6), 1159-1168. Morehead, K., Dunlosky, J., & Rawson, K. A. (2019). How much mightier is the pen than the keyboard for note-taking? Educational Psychology Review, 31, 753-780.

The Deal That Changes After You Say Yes

Once people agree to something, most of them stick with it even after learning the real cost.

Transcript

Once you say yes, you keep saying yes, even when the deal gets worse.

In nineteen seventy eight, Robert Cialdini asked students to join a psychology study.

Some were told upfront it started at seven in the morning. Only about a third agreed.

Others were first asked if they would join, and only after saying yes were told about the seven o'clock start.

More than half agreed, and almost all of them actually showed up.

This is the low ball technique. Once you commit, you feel you have to stay consistent, even when the terms change.

It is why a car price suddenly grows at the last step, or why fees appear at the checkout.

When the deal changes after you say yes, you are allowed to change your answer too.

The science

Based on the low ball technique (Cialdini et al., 1978).

Cialdini, R. B., Cacioppo, J. T., Bassett, R., & Miller, J. A. (1978). Low-ball procedure for producing compliance: Commitment then cost. JPSP, 36(5), 463-476 (31% vs 56%; 95% of low-balled participants showed up).

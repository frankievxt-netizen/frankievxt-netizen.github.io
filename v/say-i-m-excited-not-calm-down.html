Say I'm Excited, Not Calm Down

Telling yourself I am excited before a stressful moment works better than trying to calm down.

Transcript

Before a big moment, do not tell yourself to calm down. Say: I am excited.

In twenty fourteen, Alison Wood Brooks at Harvard tested this before karaoke, public speaking and math tests.

Some people said I am anxious, some said I am calm, and some said I am excited.

The excited group sang more accurately, gave more persuasive speeches and scored higher.

Anxiety and excitement feel almost the same in your body: racing heart, fast breathing, energy.

Trying to calm down fights your body. Calling it excitement just changes the label, and points it toward a chance instead of a threat.

This is called anxiety reappraisal.

Next time your heart is pounding, say it out loud: I am excited. Your body is already ready.

The science

Based on anxiety reappraisal (Brooks, 2014).

Brooks, A. W. (2014). Get excited: Reappraising pre-performance anxiety as excitement. Journal of Experimental Psychology: General, 143(3), 1144-1158. Education only, not clinical advice.

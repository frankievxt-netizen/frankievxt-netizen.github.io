Why Your Best Friends Live Closest to You

Who becomes your friend depends far more on distance than you would like to admit.

Transcript

Your closest friends may be your friends mostly because of where you lived.

In the late nineteen forties, Leon Festinger and his colleagues studied friendships in a student housing complex at MIT.

Apartments were assigned randomly, so nobody chose their neighbors.

Yet people were far more likely to befriend the person next door than someone just a few doors away.

Even the people living near stairways and mailboxes made more friends, because others passed by them every day.

This is the propinquity effect. Small daily encounters slowly turn strangers into friends.

It is why you bonded with the person at the desk next to yours, not the one across the office.

If you want new friends, change where you spend your time, not just who you message.

The science

Based on the propinquity effect (Festinger, Schachter and Back, 1950).

Festinger, L., Schachter, S., & Back, K. (1950). Social Pressures in Informal Groups: A Study of Human Factors in Housing. Westgate housing project, MIT.

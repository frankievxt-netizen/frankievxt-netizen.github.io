Why 75% Lean Sounds Healthier Than 25% Fat

The exact same meat tastes better when the label says lean instead of fat.

Transcript

The exact same meat tastes better when the label changes one word.

In nineteen eighty eight, Irwin Levin and Gary Gaeth gave people ground beef with two different labels.

One said seventy five percent lean. The other said twenty five percent fat.

It was the same beef. But people rated the lean version as better tasting, less greasy and higher quality.

This is the framing effect. Your brain reacts to how a fact is presented, not just the fact.

Doctors see it too. A surgery with a ninety percent survival rate sounds far better than one with a ten percent death rate.

Every ad, menu and headline is choosing a frame for you.

So when something sounds great, flip it around and hear it again.

The science

Based on the framing effect (Levin and Gaeth, 1988).

Levin, I. P., & Gaeth, G. J. (1988). How consumers are affected by the framing of attribute information before and after consuming the product. Journal of Consumer Research, 15(3), 374-378. Medical framing: McNeil, Pauker, Sox & Tversky (1982).

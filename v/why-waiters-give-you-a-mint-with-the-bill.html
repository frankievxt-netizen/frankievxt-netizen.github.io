Why Waiters Give You a Mint With the Bill

The mint that comes with your bill is a small psychological trick that raises tips.

Transcript

That mint with your bill is a small psychological trick.

In two thousand two, David Strohmetz and his team tested it in a real restaurant.

One mint with the bill raised tips a little. Two mints raised them by about fourteen percent.

But the biggest jump came when the waiter left one mint, walked away, then came back with a second one, just for you.

Tips rose by about twenty three percent.

This is the rule of reciprocity. When someone gives you something that feels personal, you feel a pull to give back.

It is why free samples, free gifts and free trials work so well on you.

A gift can be kind. But when it arrives right before someone asks you for something, notice the timing.

The science

Based on the reciprocity norm (Strohmetz et al., 2002).

Strohmetz, D. B., Rind, B., Fisher, R., & Lynn, M. (2002). Sweetening the till: The use of candy to increase restaurant tipping. Journal of Applied Social Psychology, 32(2), 300-309. Reciprocity as an influence principle: Cialdini, Influence (1984).

One Word Can Rewrite Your Memory

A single word in a question can change what you remember seeing.

Transcript

One word in a question can change what you remember seeing.

In nineteen seventy four, Elizabeth Loftus and John Palmer showed people a film of a car crash.

Then they asked: how fast were the cars going when they hit each other?

For some people, the word hit became smashed. Those people guessed higher speeds.

A week later, about a third of the smashed group remembered seeing broken glass. There was no broken glass.

Your memory is not a recording. Every time you recall something, your brain rebuilds it, and new details slip in.

It is why two siblings can swear they remember the same childhood fight completely differently.

So the next time you are sure about what happened, ask yourself who asked you the question.

The science

Based on the misinformation effect (Loftus and Palmer, 1974).

Loftus, E. F., & Palmer, J. C. (1974). Reconstruction of automobile destruction: An example of the interaction between language and memory. Journal of Verbal Learning and Verbal Behavior, 13(5), 585-589. Broken glass reported by 32% (smashed) vs 14% (hit).

Why You Can't Tickle Yourself

Try to tickle yourself and nothing happens, because your brain sees it coming.

Transcript

Try to tickle yourself right now. Nothing, right?

In nineteen ninety eight, Sarah-Jayne Blakemore and her colleagues built a tickling robot.

When people moved the robot themselves, the touch felt dull. When someone else moved it, it tickled.

Then they added a tiny delay between the person's movement and the touch. The tickle came back.

Your brain predicts the result of your own movements, and turns the feeling down before it arrives.

It is the same reason you do not notice your own clothes or your own breathing most of the time.

Your brain cares about surprises from the outside world, not about things it already planned.

A tickle is not about touch. It is about not knowing what comes next.

The science

Based on sensory prediction and tickling (Blakemore, Wolpert and Frith, 1998).

Blakemore, S.-J., Wolpert, D. M., & Frith, C. D. (1998). Central cancellation of self-produced tickle sensation. Nature Neuroscience, 1(7), 635-640. Delay study: Blakemore, Frith & Wolpert (1999), Journal of Cognitive Neuroscience.

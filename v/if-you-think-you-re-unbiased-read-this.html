If You Think You're Unbiased, Read This

Almost everyone believes they are less biased than the average person, and that belief is itself a bias.

Transcript

You spot bias in other people instantly. In yourself, almost never.

In two thousand two, Emily Pronin, Daniel Lin and Lee Ross described common biases to Stanford students.

Then they asked: how much does this affect you, and how much does it affect the average student?

People rated themselves as clearly less biased than everyone else.

They called it the bias blind spot. You judge others by their behavior, but you judge yourself by your intentions.

And since your intentions always feel fair, your own bias stays invisible.

It is why every argument ends with both people thinking the other one is unreasonable.

If you finished this video thinking of someone else, that is the blind spot.

The science

Based on the bias blind spot (Pronin, Lin and Ross, 2002).

Pronin, E., Lin, D. Y., & Ross, L. (2002). The bias blind spot: Perceptions of bias in self versus others. Personality and Social Psychology Bulletin, 28(3), 369-381.

Why Saying Thank You Keeps Partners Together

Feeling appreciated by your partner predicts whether you stay committed months later.

Transcript

Two words may protect a relationship more than you think: thank you.

In twenty twelve, Amie Gordon and her colleagues followed couples over several months.

People who felt more appreciated by their partner were more committed, and more likely to still be together months later.

And when one partner felt appreciated, they became more appreciative in return. It spread.

The researchers describe appreciation as a signal: I see what you do, and you matter to me.

After years together, small efforts become invisible. Making coffee, picking up groceries, remembering things.

When nobody notices them anymore, people slowly feel taken for granted.

So name one small thing your partner did today. It costs nothing, and it may keep everything.

The science

Based on gratitude in relationships (Gordon et al., 2012).

Gordon, A. M., Impett, E. A., Kogan, A., Oveis, C., & Keltner, D. (2012). To have and to hold: Gratitude promotes relationship maintenance in intimate bonds. Journal of Personality and Social Psychology, 103(2), 257-274.

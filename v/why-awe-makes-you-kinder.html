Why Awe Makes You Kinder

One minute of staring up at giant trees made people more likely to help a stranger.

Transcript

One minute looking up at giant trees can make you kinder.

In twenty fifteen, Paul Piff and his colleagues had students look up at a grove of very tall eucalyptus trees.

Another group looked at a plain building for the same amount of time.

Then a researcher stumbled and dropped a handful of pens.

The students who had looked at the trees picked up more pens.

The researchers say awe creates a small self. You feel like part of something bigger, and your own worries shrink.

It is why a starry night, an ocean, or a great song can suddenly make your problems feel lighter.

If you feel stuck in your own head today, go find something bigger than you.

The science

Based on awe and the small self (Piff et al., 2015).

Piff, P. K., Dietze, P., Feinberg, M., Stancato, D. M., & Keltner, D. (2015). Awe, the small self, and prosocial behavior. Journal of Personality and Social Psychology, 108(6), 883-899.

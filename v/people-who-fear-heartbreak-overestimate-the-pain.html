People Who Fear Heartbreak Overestimate the Pain

You consistently overestimate how bad and how long you will feel after something goes wrong.

Transcript

Scared a breakup or a rejection will hurt forever? It will not hurt as long as you think.

In nineteen ninety eight, Daniel Gilbert and his colleagues asked young professors how they would feel if they were denied tenure.

They predicted years of unhappiness.

But professors who had actually been denied were about as happy as those who got it.

This is the impact bias. You predict that bad events will hurt more and longer than they really do.

You forget about your psychological immune system. You adapt, find meaning, and focus on other things.

It works for good things too. The new phone or the promotion will not make you happy for as long as you hope.

Whatever you are dreading right now, future you is probably going to be fine.

The science

Based on the impact bias (Gilbert et al., 1998).

Gilbert, D. T., Pinel, E. C., Wilson, T. D., Blumberg, S. J., & Wheatley, T. P. (1998). Immune neglect: A source of durability bias in affective forecasting. Journal of Personality and Social Psychology, 75(3), 617-638.

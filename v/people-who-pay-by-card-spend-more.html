People Who Pay by Card Spend More

People bid much more for the same tickets when they were told they would pay by credit card.

Transcript

Paying by card makes you spend more, even for the exact same thing.

In two thousand one, Drazen Prelec and Duncan Simester ran an auction for sold out basketball tickets at MIT.

Half the bidders were told they had to pay in cash. The other half, by credit card.

The card group bid much more, in some cases around double.

Psychologists call this the pain of paying. Handing over cash feels like a real loss. Tapping a card barely registers.

It is why phone payments and one click buying are so easy, and so expensive.

And why apps turn money into coins, points and tokens.

If you want to spend less, make paying feel real again. Pay in cash for a week and notice the difference.

The science

Based on the pain of paying (Prelec and Simester, 2001; Prelec and Loewenstein, 1998).

Prelec, D., & Simester, D. (2001). Always leave home without it: A further investigation of the credit-card effect on willingness to pay. Marketing Letters, 12(1), 5-12. Prelec, D., & Loewenstein, G. (1998). The red and the black: Mental accounting of savings and debt. Marketing Science, 17(1), 4-28.

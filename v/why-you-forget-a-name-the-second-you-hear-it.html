Why You Forget a Name the Second You Hear It

You forget names right after hearing them because your brain is busy with something else.

Transcript

You forget someone's name seconds after hearing it, and it is not because you do not care.

In nineteen seventy three, Malcolm Brenner had people take turns reading words aloud in a circle.

Later, they remembered most words, except the ones read right before their own turn.

He called it the next in line effect. While you prepare what to say, your brain stops recording what others say.

That is exactly what happens in introductions. They say their name while you are planning to say yours.

So the name never gets stored in the first place. You did not forget it. You never heard it.

The fix is to say their name back right away. Nice to meet you, Sarah.

Remembering names is not a talent. It is just waiting one second before you talk.

The science

Based on the next in line effect (Brenner, 1973).

Brenner, M. (1973). The next-in-line effect. Journal of Verbal Learning and Verbal Behavior, 12(3), 320-323. Replicated by Bond, Pitre & Van Leeuwen (1991).

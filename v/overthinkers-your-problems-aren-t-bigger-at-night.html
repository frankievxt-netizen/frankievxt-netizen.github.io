Overthinkers, Your Problems Aren't Bigger at Night

Problems feel bigger at night not because they changed, but because your tired brain reacts more strongly.

Transcript

Your problems are not bigger at night. Your brain is just reading them differently.

In two thousand seven, Matthew Walker and his colleagues kept people awake for a full night, then showed them upsetting images.

The amygdala, the brain's alarm center, reacted about sixty percent more strongly than in people who had slept.

And its link to the calm, logical part of the brain was weaker.

In twenty twenty two, sleep researchers proposed the mind after midnight hypothesis. Late at night, thinking may lean negative and impulsive.

It is still a hypothesis. But it matches what most people feel at two in the morning.

The worry that felt like a disaster at night often looks manageable over breakfast.

If a thought feels huge after midnight, do not decide anything. Let the morning version of you take a look.

The science

Based on sleep loss and emotional reactivity (Yoo et al., 2007); the mind after midnight hypothesis (Tubbs et al., 2022).

Why Believing Someone Likes You Makes It True

Simply believing someone likes you changes how you act, and that makes them actually like you.

Transcript

If you believe someone likes you, they probably will.

In nineteen eighty six, Rebecca Curtis and Kim Miller paired up students for a short chat.

Before it started, some were secretly told their partner liked them. Others were told their partner did not.

The ones who thought they were liked acted warmer. They shared more, disagreed less and sounded friendlier.

And their partners ended up liking them more, even though the original story was made up.

This is a self fulfilling prophecy. Your expectation changes your behavior, and your behavior creates the result.

If you walk into a room expecting to be rejected, you act guarded, and people pull away.

So walk in assuming they like you. Most of the time, you will turn out right.

The science

Based on reciprocal liking and self fulfilling prophecy (Curtis and Miller, 1986).

Curtis, R. C., & Miller, K. (1986). Believing another likes or dislikes you: Behaviors making the beliefs come true. Journal of Personality and Social Psychology, 51(2), 284-290.

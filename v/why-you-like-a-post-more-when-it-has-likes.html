Why You Like a Post More When It Has Likes

Teens were more likely to like a photo when it already had many likes, and their brain reward areas lit up more.

Transcript

You are more likely to like a post that already has lots of likes.

In twenty sixteen, Lauren Sherman and her colleagues at UCLA built a fake social app for teenagers inside a brain scanner.

Teens saw photos, some of them their own, with either many or few likes. The likes were randomly assigned.

They were much more likely to like photos that already had many likes, no matter what the picture was.

And when their own photos had many likes, reward areas in their brains became more active.

This is social proof in your pocket. The number tells your brain what to think before you decide.

It works on adults too. Popular posts get more popular, quality aside.

Next time you tap like, ask yourself: do I actually like this, or do I like that others liked it?

The science

Based on social media likes and conformity (Sherman et al., 2016).

Sherman, L. E., Payton, A. A., Hernandez, L. M., Greenfield, P. M., & Dapretto, M. (2016). The power of the like in adolescence: Effects of peer influence on neural and behavioral responses to social media. Psychological Science, 27(7), 1027-1035.

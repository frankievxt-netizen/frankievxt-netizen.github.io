Almost No Conversation Ends When You Want It To

Almost no conversation ends when both people want it to, and you are probably wrong about which way.

Transcript

Almost no conversation ends when both people want it to.

In twenty twenty one, Adam Mastroianni and his colleagues asked people when they had wanted their last conversation to end.

Then they paired strangers in a lab, let them talk, and asked each person privately.

Only about two percent of conversations ended when both people wanted.

Often one wanted to leave and the other wanted to keep going. And people had no idea what their partner wanted.

Their guesses about the other person were off by roughly half the conversation's length.

The problem is that ending a chat feels rude, so everyone hides their signal.

So next time, just say it kindly. I have to go, but this was great. Both of you will be relieved.

The science

Based on conversation length mismatch (Mastroianni et al., 2021).

Mastroianni, A. M., Gilbert, D. T., Cooney, G., & Wilson, T. D. (2021). Do conversations end when people want them to? PNAS, 118(10), e2011809118.

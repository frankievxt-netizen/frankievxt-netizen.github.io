The Stanford Prison Experiment Was Partly Staged

The most famous experiment in psychology may not show what you were taught it shows.

Transcript

The most famous experiment in psychology may not prove what you were taught.

In nineteen seventy one, Philip Zimbardo turned a Stanford basement into a fake prison, with students as guards and prisoners.

The story says the guards quickly turned cruel on their own, and the study had to stop after six days.

But in twenty nineteen, researcher Thibault Le Texier published what he found in the archives.

Recordings show guards were encouraged to be tough. One prisoner later said his famous breakdown was partly acted.

Zimbardo defended his work, but many psychologists now see it as a demonstration, not a clean experiment.

Bad situations can push people toward cruelty. But people also follow instructions, and play the role they think is wanted.

Sometimes the most famous lesson is the one we should question most.

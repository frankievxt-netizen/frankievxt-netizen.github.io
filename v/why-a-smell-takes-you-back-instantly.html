Why a Smell Takes You Back Instantly

A single smell can drop you back into a childhood moment faster than any photo.

Transcript

One smell can take you back twenty years in a second.

Psychologists call it the Proust effect, after a writer who described a cake bringing back his childhood.

In two thousand, Simon Chu and John Downes found that memories triggered by smell tend to come from early childhood.

Rachel Herz found smell memories also feel more emotional than memories triggered by pictures or words.

One reason is wiring. Smell signals reach brain areas for emotion and memory more directly than your other senses.

That is why a stranger's perfume can suddenly make you think of someone you have not seen in years.

Or why your grandmother's kitchen comes back the moment you smell fresh bread.

Your nose remembers things you thought you forgot.

The science

Based on odor evoked memory (Chu and Downes, 2000; Herz and Schooler, 2002).

Chu, S., & Downes, J. J. (2000). Long live Proust: The odour-cued autobiographical memory bump. Cognition, 75(2), B41-B50. Herz, R. S., & Schooler, J. W. (2002). A naturalistic study of autobiographical memories evoked by olfactory and visual cues. American Journal of Psychology, 115(1), 21-32.

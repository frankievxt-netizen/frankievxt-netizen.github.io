People Who Hate Waiting for Results

People were more stressed by a fifty percent chance of pain than by knowing pain was certain.

Transcript

If waiting for news stresses you more than bad news would, you are not imagining it.

In twenty sixteen, Archy de Berker and his team at University College London had people play a game where rocks might hide snakes.

A snake meant a small electric shock. The odds changed as they played.

Stress was measured through sweat, pupil size and self reports.

Stress peaked when the chance of a shock was around fifty percent, not when a shock was certain.

Uncertainty keeps your brain on alert, constantly preparing for both outcomes.

It is why waiting for test results, or a reply after a job interview, can feel worse than hearing bad news.

When you can, ask for the answer. Even a hard truth lets your mind finally rest.

The science

Based on uncertainty and stress (de Berker et al., 2016).

de Berker, A. O., Rutledge, R. B., Mathys, C., Marshall, L., Cross, G. F., Dolan, R. J., & Bestmann, S. (2016). Computations of uncertainty mediate acute stress responses in humans. Nature Communications, 7, 10996.

Power Posing Doesn't Change Your Hormones

The famous power pose was said to change your hormones, but bigger studies could not find that effect.

Transcript

Stand like a superhero for two minutes and your hormones change. That was the claim.

In twenty ten, Dana Carney, Amy Cuddy and Andy Yap reported that expansive poses raised testosterone and lowered stress hormones.

It became one of the most watched talks of all time.

But in twenty fifteen, Eva Ranehill and her team tested it with many more people. No hormone changes.

Dana Carney, the lead author, later said she no longer believes the effect is real.

Some studies still find people feel a little more confident after an open posture. That part may be real.

But your body is not a switch, and two minutes does not rewrite your chemistry.

Confidence comes from preparation, not posture.

The science

Based on power posing (Carney, Cuddy and Yap, 2010; Ranehill et al., 2015).
